# Revised figure captions

**Figure 1. Ordinary pooled and equal-sequence-standardized group contrasts in the Age IAT archive.** Each row is one eligible pairwise comparison; panels separate the four outcomes. Open circles show the ordinary pooled contrast and filled squares the equal-sequence-standardized contrast, joined by a light connecting segment; horizontal lines are 95% stratified-bootstrap confidence intervals based on 3,000 replicates stratified by grouping category and block-derived sequence. The two estimators were nearly identical because sequence allocation was close to balanced within groups; the largest observed absolute standardization shift was 0.00071 score units. Equal-sequence standardization did not reduce root mean squared error at any sample size examined.

**Figure 2. Group-specific sequence contrasts of the reported IAT outcomes.** Gamma is half the difference between the congruent-first and incongruent-first group means of the reported score. Points are group-specific estimates and horizontal lines are 95% stratified-bootstrap confidence intervals. A dagger marks a group involved in at least one pairwise difference in gamma that remained significant after false-discovery-rate correction. Across all families and outcomes, 24 of 56 pairwise comparisons were significant after correction and the largest absolute difference in gamma was 0.04223. These are pairwise bootstrap contrasts, not omnibus interaction tests. Gamma describes sequence sensitivity of the reported score and should not be interpreted as a pure position, practice, or carryover effect.
