# Group-comparison strengthening audit

## Executive decision

The completed analyses support a methodological submission focused on exact estimands, sequence heterogeneity, and finite-sample operating characteristics. They do not support a claim that sequence allocation broadly distorts substantive IAT group comparisons.

## Benchmark reproduction

All 18 prespecified benchmarks reproduced within tolerance. Gender–Science retained 133,460 participants, including 67,274 congruent-first and 66,186 incongruent-first participants. Age retained 147,296 participants, including 74,347 congruent-first and 72,949 incongruent-first participants.

## Public score reconstruction

The independently reconstructed public Age score used recorded latencies, the documented fast-response screen, and no second 600-ms penalty. Across 133,267 screened participants, agreement with the supplied score was essentially exact: Pearson r = 0.999999995, median difference = 0, RMSE = 0.0000355, and 99.98% of values agreed within 0.001. Adding 600 ms again produced systematic disagreement and is retained only as a double-penalty diagnostic.

## Linkage and sequence validation

The participant file matched 147,285 of 147,296 analytic Age participants. Eleven participants had no participant-file record. Participant-file Order was documented for 146,599 participants and agreed with the independently derived block sequence in every case. The 686 participants with blank Order retained valid block-derived sequence coding and remained in the analysis.

## Observed allocation consequences

Observed sequence allocation was close to balanced within eligible groups. The largest between-group allocation difference was 0.01334, the largest absolute allocation component was 0.000617 score units, and the largest pairwise standardization shift was 0.000712 score units. One observed sign change and one interval-conclusion change occurred at age-band boundaries; neither persisted when approximate age was reduced by one year.

## Sequence heterogeneity

Pairwise differences in the sequence contrast were evaluated using stratified-bootstrap inference with false-discovery-rate correction. Twenty-four of 56 pairwise comparisons were significant after correction, and the largest absolute difference in the sequence contrast was 0.04223. These comparisons are pairwise contrasts, not omnibus interaction tests. The sequence contrast describes sensitivity of the reported score and is not interpreted as a pure position, practice, or carryover effect.

## Finite-sample operating characteristics

Empirical resampling used 5,000 replicates per condition with seed 20260801. Mean sign-disagreement rates decreased from 4.9% at 50 participants per group to 1.5% at 1,000 participants per group. Interval-conclusion disagreement remained near 2%. The highest disagreement rates occurred for population contrasts near zero. Equal-sequence standardization did not reduce root mean squared error at any sample size.

## Age-band sensitivity

Approximate age was calculated from collection year minus documented birth year. Repeating the analysis after subtracting one year moved 14,424 participants between bands but preserved all four eligible age groups and the number of FDR-significant sequence-contrast comparisons. The isolated sign and interval-conclusion changes did not persist.

## Limitations

Substantive subgroup analyses were conducted in the Age archive because participant-level Gender–Science demographic metadata were unavailable in the analysis environment. Country comparisons were not performed because the documented country fields were empty for the 2019 Age collection. Approximate age could differ from chronological age by one year because birth date was unavailable.

## Classification

The analysis is ready for a methodological submission. Its contribution is the exact identification of the equal-sequence marginal, sequence contrast, and allocation component; the documentation of group-specific sequence sensitivity; and the comparison of pooled and equal-sequence estimators under observed and finite-sample conditions.
